USE CASE · CAREER

Deciding on a career change, across a month of ordinary evenings.

Whether to take the new role. Whether to leave the job that is wearing you down. Whether to finally start the thing you have been talking about for two years. Counsel.day gives the question a duration you set, takes one private vote each evening, and opens the verdict on the final day.

01 · WHAT IS IN SCOPE

Five questions sitting on the same fault line.

Should I take the new role. Should I leave the job that is wearing me down. Should I start the thing I have been talking about for two years. Should I retrain. Should I go contracting.

They differ in the detail and agree in the structure. Each one trades a life you already know for a life you are estimating. Each one gets judged on a day when you are either tired or fresh, and rarely on both. And each one carries a deadline set by somebody else: an offer that expires, a notice period, an enrolment date, a contract that ends in March.

Counsel.day does not tell you which to take. It gives the question a duration, collects one private vote from each participant every evening, and hands the pattern back when the period closes.

02 · SOLO OR COUPLE

A career decision is usually a partnership decision in disguise.

The offer arrives addressed to one person. The consequences do not. A new role changes who does the school run, whose evenings disappear, whose income carries the household through the first six months, and which of you gets to be the tired one.

So the rule is plain. Run it as a Couple decision when a second person is materially affected. Run it Solo when they are not. If you cannot tell which it is, that uncertainty is itself worth putting to a vote.

Running it as a Couple also removes the sequencing problem: the one where you decide privately, then present the conclusion, and the conversation becomes a negotiation against a position you already hold. In a Couple decision neither of you can see the other's votes until the close, so neither of you spends four weeks arguing with a decision that is already made.

Family works the same way, for up to six participants, when adult children or a wider household are carrying part of the consequence.

03 · HOW LONG TO RUN IT

Thirty days suits most career decisions.

Thirty days is the default here for a reason. It is long enough to cross a whole work cycle: the Monday when the job is fine, the Wednesday when it is not, a month-end, a difficult meeting, and a week where the thing you would be leaving behind reminds you why you stayed this long.

Shorten to twenty-one days when there is a live offer with an expiry date on it, or fourteen when the deadline is genuinely that tight. Stretch to sixty when the question is retraining or a change of field, where the consequences run for years and the enthusiasm has seasons of its own.

Durations run from seven to ninety days. A live decision can be extended up to ninety if the offer date moves; it cannot be shortened.

04 · WHAT THE VERDICT PRODUCES

What the final evening hands you.

Each participant's trajectory. Firming, drifting, or steady, with the evening where it turned. A yes that strengthened across four weeks is a different fact from a yes on the night the offer landed in your inbox.

The themes your notes actually returned to. Money is one of them. It is rarely the loudest. Autonomy, status, the commute, the person you would stop working with, the version of yourself you had in mind at twenty-five: these show up in the notes whether or not you set out to write about them.

Where the two of you diverged, if there are two of you. Often it is not the job at all. It is what the job stands in for: security, ambition, or how much of the week belongs to the household.

One specific question to open the conversation that follows. Built from the words you both wrote across the period, not from a template.

Counsel.day is a record and an analysis, not an adviser. It does not assess the offer, rate the employer, or recommend a path. It shows you plainly what each of you thought across the whole period, rather than on the one evening you happened to talk about it.

05 · RUNNING IT ALONE

Solo, when the only person it lands on is you.

The mechanism does not change when there is one of you. One vote each evening, four buttons, an optional note that you can type or speak. There is nobody to compare against, which is rather the point: you are not measuring yourself against a partner, you are measuring this Thursday against the three Thursdays before it.

At the close you get your own trajectory, the themes your own notes carried, and one question to sit with before you act. Plenty of career questions never needed a second voter; they needed thirty honest evenings and something that remembered all of them.
