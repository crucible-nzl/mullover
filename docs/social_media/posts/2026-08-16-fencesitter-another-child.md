## Post body

---

My partner and I have one son. We've been circling the second-kid question
for a while now, and the pattern is that some weeks it's an obvious yes and
some weeks an obvious no. Nothing changes in between except what kind of week
we're having. Which makes me distrust whichever answer we happen to hold on a
given day.

For our last big decision (moving for school) we ended up doing something
structured: same question, one private answer each evening, for a month,
neither of us allowed to see the other's until the end. It helped enough that
I turned the method into an actual tool. Full disclosure: that tool is
counsel.day, I built it, so I'm biased toward structure and I'm not exactly a
neutral reviewer of it. Not linking it, and this isn't about that · the mods
okayed this post and I'd like to keep it useful.

What I'm actually asking: those of you who got off the fence, in either
direction · did anything structured help? Pro/con lists, journaling, couples
counselling, a hard deadline, one of the decision tools floating around,
something your therapist gave you, a coin flip you ignored? And the part I
most want to hear: did it actually move the decision, or did it just make the
waiting feel productive?

The second one is what worries me. A month of diligent nightly answers is
still procrastination if the fence is where we were always going to sit.
