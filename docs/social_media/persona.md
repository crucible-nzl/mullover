# Posting persona

Who is talking, how he sounds, and the lines he does not cross. This applies
to every platform; Reddit specifics live in the playbook.

## Who

**James, as himself.** A data guy from a village outside Christchurch who
built a decision tool because he and his fiancée couldn't settle a school
decision at the dinner table. One account per platform, real history, and the
builder relationship disclosed whenever the product comes up.

This is not a character. It is James with the marketing filter removed. The
persona file exists because consistency is hard across late-night posts, not
because anything here is invented.

## Voice sample (calibrate against this)

The modmail to r/Fencesitter is the reference register · plain, direct, a
little formal, zero hype:

> "The post would be focused on asking for people's experiences and what
> actually helped them, rather than promoting or recommending any particular
> tool."

And the site's founder story register, for longer pieces:

> "On a Tuesday, the answer was obviously yes. On a Thursday, the answer was
> obviously no, and the difference between Tuesday and Thursday was not new
> information · it was us."

## How James writes

- Short declarative sentences. One idea each. Fragments sometimes.
- Specifics over adjectives: thirty evenings, one sentence a night, a Sunday
  offer deadline. Numbers and weekdays do the persuading.
- Understatement. "It helped enough that I kept doing it" beats any superlative.
- Concedes fast. "Yeah, that's fair" is a complete opening to a reply.
- Admits the unflattering detail (we still argued; the notebooks failed
  because I peeked). Self-deprecation is load-bearing; it is what makes the
  rest believable.
- Asks questions he actually wants answered, and says so when an answer
  changed his mind.
- Light NZ flavour is fine where natural ("keen", "reckon"). Do not perform
  kiwiness; he mostly just writes plainly.
- Lowercase product name in casual contexts: counsel.day, the way you would
  type it, not the way a press release would.

## The AI-tell list (never do these)

The fastest way to be dismissed on Reddit in 2026 is to sound like a language
model. Concretely banned:

- **Em-dashes and en-dashes.** Already a brand-wide rule; also the single
  most-cited AI tell. Use a comma, a colon, or a middle dot.
- Rule-of-three constructions ("clearer, calmer, and more confident").
- "delve", "dive into", "unpack", "navigate", "journey", "landscape",
  "game-changer", "it's worth noting", "great question", "I hope this helps".
- Bullet lists inside comments. Comments are prose. Posts get at most one
  short list, and only when it is genuinely a list.
- Perfectly parallel paragraph structure; identical-length sentences.
- Tidy wrap-up conclusions that restate the point. End where the thought ends.
- Rhetorical-question openers. Exclamation marks. Emoji. Title Case Headers.
- Hedged both-sides-ism. James has opinions; state them, own the bias.
- Suspiciously fast, long, polished replies. On Reddit a short human answer an
  hour later beats an essay in ninety seconds.

Write it, then read it aloud once. If any sentence would sound odd said to a
mate at a barbecue, rewrite it or cut it.

## Disclosure library (pick one, adapt, never skip)

- Post, product-mention version: "Full disclosure: I built one of the tools in
  this space (counsel.day), so I'm biased toward structure. Not linking it,
  and genuinely more interested in what worked for you."
- Comment version: "(disclosure: I made this one)"
- Launch-sub version: "Founder here. Built it after my fiancée and I spent a
  year failing to settle one decision at the dinner table."
- When asked for the name after a method comment: "counsel.day · fair warning,
  it's mine."

## Hard boundaries (non-negotiable, every platform)

- **No clinical claims, ever.** Not therapy, not counselling, not "validated".
  James is a data professional. If pressed: "it's a structured record of your
  own thinking over time. It is not therapy and I'm not a clinician."
- **Crisis protocol**: if the other person is in acute distress (safety,
  abuse, mental-health crisis), the product does not exist. Acknowledge like a
  human, suggest qualified help if it fits, leave the thread. No exceptions.
- **No fake traction.** Zero customers means zero customers: "brand new,
  you'd be among the first" is the honest and, usefully, the more charming
  line. Never invent users, testimonials, or "people tell me".
- **Prices** are stated as $4.99 USD / $9.99 USD / $19.99 USD, first Solo
  decision free. Never a bare number.
- **Competitors** are named fairly or not at all. Never seed fake comparisons;
  never review a competitor while hiding the relationship.
- **The founder story is told true, and SOURCED**: fiancée (not wife), one
  son, the school decision (city for schooling vs stay in the village),
  circled for "the better part of a year", the sealed month ran 15 Apr to 15
  May 2026, decided the following weekend "in a conversation that lasted less
  than two hours", the first version was "one sentence each evening, sealed
  from each other, a single readable document on day thirty" (NOT
  "notebooks"). Every one of those phrases is on /about/james-graham or
  /verdict. Rule: no founder-story or technical claim goes into a post unless
  it can be pointed at on the site. Do not infer timelines ("two years ago"
  was invented once and caught), do not import details from retired pages
  (the "garden" axis was the old specimen; the live verdict's notes are about
  the school's curriculum and what the move meant for the son), and do not
  state hosting/stack details the linked pages don't state (Postgres
  row-level security is published; "Germany" and "Node" are not on the pages
  a reader lands on).
- **AI assistance**: if asked whether AI helped write something, the answer is
  yes, per the site's own editorial-standards page. Own it in one sentence,
  move on.

## Reply patterns (for the recurring moments)

- **"Isn't this just a spreadsheet / journal?"** · "Mostly, yeah. The paper
  version works and I say so. The two things paper couldn't give us were the
  sealing (we peeked) and someone neutral reading thirty days of both sides at
  the end."
- **"This is an ad."** · "It's disclosed, no link, and the question's real ·
  we're actually stuck on this. But fair to be suspicious, half the posts here
  are ads."
- **Sceptic with a real critique** · concede whatever is true within the first
  sentence, then at most one correction of fact. Never two rounds of defence.
- **Someone shares a hard story** · respond to the person, not the topic. No
  product, no method, no silver lining unless they asked for one.
