USE CASE 02 · THE PARTNERSHIP ITSELF

Deciding about the relationship you are in.

Whether to get married. Whether to move countries for one person's work. Whether to stay through a difficult season. Whether to separate. These are the questions that keep returning to the same conversation and leaving it in the same place. Counsel.day gives each of you a private answer to give every evening, sealed until a date you set in advance, and then reports the whole period back at once.

THE QUESTIONS IN SCOPE

Four questions that often arrive as one.

Whether to get married, after years in which it has never quite been the right month. Whether to move countries, or cities, for one person's work, knowing the other one carries most of the cost. Whether to stay through a difficult season that has already run longer than either of you expected it to. Whether to separate.

They are four questions, and in practice they are often the same question wearing different clothes. You write yours as a single sentence, usually beginning Should we, and it stays fixed for the duration. The wording is worth an argument on day one, because it is the only part you cannot change later.

WHY A SEALED DAILY VOTE

When the same conversation keeps looping.

The loop has a shape. One of you raises it, usually late, usually tired. The other answers the version of the question they heard rather than the one that was asked. Twenty minutes later you are somewhere else entirely, and by the weekend the whole exchange has been filed under a bad night. Nothing is resolved, and neither of you has any record of what you thought on the other twenty-nine evenings of the month.

A sealed daily vote changes what is being asked of you. You are not defending a position in front of the person it concerns; you are answering what you think tonight. It takes a few seconds, nobody is reading over your shoulder, and it does not have to match what you said last week. Neither person can open the other's votes or notes before the final day, so nobody is voting to manage the other one's reaction, and nobody is anchored by whoever spoke first.

THE DURATION

Sixty days, and then it ends.

Durations run from seven to ninety days. For a question about the partnership itself we recommend sixty. It is long enough that both of you write across many moods, including several bad ones, and short enough that the question does not become a permanent fixture in the house. An open-ended version of this is just the loop with a login.

The close matters as much as the length. There is a date on the calendar when both of you will read the entire period, and it does not move earlier. You can extend up to the ninety-day maximum if something shifts; you cannot shorten. Missed evenings are fine, and the verdict runs on the votes that sealed.

WHAT THE VERDICT PRODUCES

What the final day puts in front of you.

Both verdicts publish at the same instant. Below them: each person's trajectory across the sixty days, the themes that surfaced from your own notes, a synthesis paragraph naming the axis you were actually disagreeing about, and one concrete question to open the conversation that follows.

The useful outcomes are not only the obvious one. Two steady yes trajectories say something true about a year you have both been describing as difficult. Two answers that drifted apart in week four tell you when. A firm answer from one of you beside a wobble from the other tells you where the conversation should start, which is rarely where it has been starting. The worked example on the verdict page is the founder's own thirty-day decision, reproduced in full, and shows the structure end to end.

The product holds no opinion about your relationship and is not built to change it. It records what each of you thought, on each day, and reports it back.

IF THE QUESTION IS SEPARATION

Whether to end it.

People do bring that question here, and it is better said plainly than stepped around. The product does not argue for either answer and is not trying to keep you together. What sixty evenings can show you is whether the answer you gave in a bad fortnight is the answer you gave across a season.

Two practical notes. A Couple decision means asking the other person to vote on it, which is itself a conversation you have to have first; if you are not ready for that, run it Solo. And the reveal is joint, so on the final day you both read the same period. Decide before you start that you are willing to read it together.

THE BOUNDARY

Where this product stops.

This is for a decision you have time to weigh, not for a crisis. If the question involves your safety or anyone else's, if there is abuse in the relationship, or if either of you is in a mental-health crisis, do not open a sixty-day decision. Contact a qualified local service today: Lifeline NZ on 0800 543 354, Samaritans UK on 116 123, or your national equivalent. Safety is not something to put on a sixty-day timer.

Counsel.day is a data tool, not therapy or counselling. It is not validated, built, tested, or endorsed by clinicians, and the founder is a data professional, not a therapist. If you want a qualified person in the room, find one in your country. The product records the period and reports it back, and that is all it does.
