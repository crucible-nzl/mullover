USE CASE · HOME AND PLACE

Deciding where to live, without deciding it on a Tuesday.

Whether to move into the city. Whether to buy the house you saw on Sunday. Whether to live nearer your parents, or further from them. Counsel.day gives the question a duration you set, takes one private vote from each of you every evening, and opens every verdict at the same instant on the final day.

01 · WHAT IS IN SCOPE

Five questions that are really one question.

Should we move into the city. Should we buy the house we saw on Sunday. Should we move closer to my parents, or a little further away. Should we take the place that adds an hour to the commute and comes with a garden. Should we move countries for her job.

Different circumstances, one shape. Each is a question about where and how you live, each lands on everyone in the household, and each arrives with a clock attached that belongs to somebody else: a vendor, a landlord, a school term, a visa, an offer that closes on Friday.

Counsel.day treats all of them the same way. You write the question as a single sentence, choose a duration, and every participant votes privately each evening until the period closes. Nothing is readable by anyone, including us, before the final day.

02 · WHY THE ANSWER KEEPS MOVING

Obviously yes on Tuesday. Obviously no on Thursday.

This is the class of decision that sentence was written for. Tuesday is the evening after the viewing, when the light in the kitchen was better than you remembered and you could already see where the table would go. Thursday is the evening you walked your own street in the rain and could not name one thing you wanted to leave behind.

The difference between Tuesday and Thursday is rarely new information. It is you, on a different evening, with a different week behind you. Both readings are honest. Neither one is the answer.

A conversation at the kitchen table captures whichever evening you happened to have it on. Thirty evenings capture the shape.

03 · HOW LONG TO RUN IT

Fourteen days under a deadline. Thirty to forty-five when the question is open.

Set fourteen days when there is a real-estate deadline or an offer already on the table. A fortnight is short, but it gives you fourteen separate evenings instead of one.

Set thirty to forty-five days when nothing is forcing the timing: when you are weighing a suburb, a city, a country, or the idea of moving at all. Six weeks carries you across a couple of pay cycles, a bad week at work, and at least one good weekend.

Durations run from seven to ninety days. You can extend a live decision up to the ninety-day maximum if the timeline slips; you cannot shorten it. The patience the duration imposes is the point of it.

04 · WHAT THE FINAL DAY PRODUCES

Four things a kitchen-table conversation cannot show you.

On the final evening every verdict publishes at once. Underneath sits an analysis of everything each of you wrote across the period.

Each person's trajectory. Whether you firmed toward yes, drifted toward no, or held steady, and where the turn happened. A conviction that rose across four weeks is different information from a yes spoken once over dinner.

The themes your notes kept returning to. Not what you predicted on day one. The subjects that actually recurred in the sentences you tapped out before bed: the commute, the garden, the distance to your mother, the street you would be leaving.

Where you actually diverged. Couples often arrive believing they disagree about the house. Read across a month of private notes, the divergence is frequently somewhere else: what one of you means by home, or what each of you is willing to give up to get it.

One specific conversation to have next. Not "communicate more openly." A concrete question built from what you each wrote, to open the conversation that comes after the verdict.

What the verdict does not do is tell you whether to buy, what you can afford, or what any property is worth. Counsel.day holds no view on any housing market and gives no financial advice. It records what each person thought, evening after evening, and reports the pattern back. The deciding stays with you.

05 · A DECISION OF THIS EXACT SHAPE

The city, the village, and a verdict with a quiet grief.

The founder ran a question of this kind on the product before anyone else did: whether to move into the city for school, or stay in the village and homeschool for another two years. Thirty days, two participants, opened on 15 April 2026 and sealed on 15 May.

Both partners moved toward yes, and neither moved at the same rate. James finished at yes, with conviction up 28 per cent across the period. Alexandra finished at lean yes, up 14 per cent. The themes that surfaced most often in the notes were the child's social world, the loss of place, and whether the school's curriculum actually fitted him.

The synthesis named the axis underneath, and it was not the house or the commute: what does each of you owe the village you have built, and what does each of you owe the child you are raising inside it. The verdict was a yes, with a quiet grief that the yes did not cancel.
