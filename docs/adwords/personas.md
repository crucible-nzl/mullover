# Google Ads personas · 20 people and what they actually type

The site's questions ("Should I leave the job I have for the contract on the
table?") are written in Counsel.day's voice. Nobody Googles in that voice.
People Google short, messy, and mid-feeling: "should i quit my job",
"cant decide between two jobs", "second baby quiz". This file rewrites the
scenario list as the searches real people run, one persona per ad group.

Reading the query lists:
- **[tool]** · explicitly wants an interactive thing (quiz, test, app,
  calculator). Highest conversion intent for us · they are asking for a
  product, and we are one. Bid highest here.
- **[deliberate]** · "how to decide", "pros and cons of". Mid intent ·
  wants a method. Our exact pitch, slightly earlier in the journey.
- **[feeling]** · "i hate my job but it pays well". Emotional, cheap clicks,
  weakest close rate. Test small.

Volume/cost labels are directional gut-calls to structure first bids ·
replace with Keyword Planner numbers before spending real money.

## Tier 1 · Couples (the core product)

### 1 · The second-child fencesitters
Mid-30s couple, one child, three years of circling. The exact audience of
the Reddit post, arriving via Google instead.
- Queries: "should we have another baby" [deliberate·high], "second baby
  quiz" [tool], "how to decide on a second child" [deliberate], "one and
  done or two" [deliberate], "i want another baby but my husband doesn't"
  [feeling·high], "fencesitter second child" [deliberate]
- Land on: `/deciding-to-have-a-child`
- Angle: the answer changes week to week · stop trusting whichever week
  you're in. Thirty sealed evenings, then the pattern.

### 2 · The first-child fencesitters
Early 30s, everyone around them is deciding, they can't.
- Queries: "do i want kids quiz" [tool·high], "should i have a baby quiz"
  [tool], "how to know if you want children" [deliberate·high], "am i too
  selfish to have kids" [feeling], "fencesitter meaning kids" [deliberate]
- Land on: `/deciding-to-have-a-child`
- Angle: a quiz gives you a stranger's scoring. This gives you your own
  answer, sampled thirty times.

### 3 · The buy-or-keep-renting couple
Pre-approval in hand, terrified of both answers.
- Queries: "should we buy a house now or wait" [deliberate·high·pricey],
  "buying vs renting how to decide" [deliberate], "house buying cold feet"
  [feeling], "am i ready to buy a house quiz" [tool]
- Land on: `/deciding-where-to-live`
- Angle: the Sunday open home changes your answer every week. Fourteen
  sealed days fits an offer deadline.
- Care: never affordability language · we hold no view on any market
  (page already says so). Negative-match "calculator", "mortgage rates".

### 4 · The move-in-together couple
Eight months in, two leases ending at different times.
- Queries: "should we move in together" [deliberate·high], "how soon is too
  soon to move in" [deliberate], "moving in together quiz" [tool], "am i
  ready to live with my boyfriend" [feeling]
- Land on: `/deciding-about-the-relationship`
- Angle: decide it on purpose, not because a lease ended.

### 5 · The relationship crossroads (early)
Two years in, keeps almost ending it, keeps not.
- Queries: "should i break up with my boyfriend quiz" [tool·very high],
  "how to know when to break up" [deliberate·high], "on and off
  relationship should i end it" [feeling], "break up or stay together test"
  [tool]
- Land on: `/deciding-about-the-relationship`
- Angle: you've had this conversation with yourself fifty times. Write it
  down once a night instead, sealed, and read the month.
- Care: BetterHelp and its cousins bid these terms up; expect pricey
  clicks. Crisis negatives apply (see README).

### 6 · The quiet marriage question
Married a decade, not in crisis, carrying the question alone.
- Queries: "how to decide whether to stay married" [deliberate], "should i
  get a divorce quiz" [tool·high·pricey], "unhappy marriage but nothing
  wrong" [feeling], "divorce or stay for the kids" [feeling·high]
- Land on: `/deciding-about-the-relationship` (its separation section is
  written for exactly this reader)
- Angle: sixty evenings tells you whether a bad fortnight or a settled
  answer is talking. Run it Solo if you're not ready to ask them to vote.
- Care: strongest boundary language required in copy · not counselling,
  not a substitute. Negative-match every lawyer/custody term.

### 7 · The emigration household
One partner has the offer abroad; the other has the parents here.
- Queries: "should we move overseas" [deliberate], "moving to australia
  pros and cons" [deliberate·high·NZ], "relocating for work spouse unhappy"
  [feeling], "how to decide to emigrate" [deliberate], "expat regret"
  [feeling]
- Land on: `/deciding-where-to-live`
- Angle: the 60-90 day duration matches visa timelines · decide across a
  season, not in the week the offer expires.

### 8 · The retire-and-downsize couple
Early 60s, the house is too big and full of everything.
- Queries: "should we downsize our house" [deliberate], "sell the family
  home and rent" [deliberate], "when to downsize after retirement"
  [deliberate], "downsizing regrets" [feeling]
- Land on: `/deciding-where-to-live`
- Angle: readiness, not affordability. The house question is really a
  question about what the next twenty years are for.
- Care: same no-financial-advice line as persona 3.

## Tier 2 · Solo deciders (free tier is the hook)

### 9 · The job-offer juggler
Offer letter open in one tab, current job in the other. The single

- Queries: "should i take the job offer" [deliberate·high], "how to decide

  [tool·very high], "take the job or stay" [deliberate], "job offer gut
  feeling wrong" [feeling]
- Land on: `/deciding-to-change-career`
- Angle: your first Solo decision is free · seven evenings before the
  deadline beats one anxious Sunday.

### 10 · The promotion-means-moving one

### 13 · The back-to-study one

## Tier 3 · Families (highest price, fewest searches)

### 18 · The school choosers
The founder's own decision, productised.
- Queries: "public vs private school how to decide" [deliberate·high],
  "should we change schools" [deliberate], "moving for school zones worth
  it" [deliberate·NZ/AU], "choosing a school for my child" [deliberate·high]
- Land on: `/verdict` · the real schooling verdict IS the message-match
  page; nothing on the internet competes with "here is the actual verdict
  from this exact decision."

### 19 · The aging-parent siblings
Three adults, one mother, four opinions.
- Queries: "should mum move in with us" [deliberate], "moving elderly
  parent closer" [deliberate·high], "siblings disagree about parents care"
  [feeling], "how to decide about aged care" [deliberate]
- Land on: `/family`
- Angle: Family tier's sealed votes mean nobody steamrolls, including the
  sibling who talks most. Gentle copy; this space grieves.

### 20 · The get-a-dog household
Kids campaigning daily, parents split.
- Queries: "should we get a dog" [deliberate·very high], "am i ready for a
  dog quiz" [tool·high], "getting a dog pros and cons" [deliberate·high]
- Land on: homepage for now · **landing-page gap**, see README. Highest
  search volume on this entire list and the lightest emotional weight ·
  ideal cheap-click testing ground for ad copy.

## Deliberately NOT advertised (from the original 25)

These stay great product uses and future guide topics, but as PAID
keywords they're excluded on policy or boundary grounds:

- **Surgery / prescribed medication decisions** · Google Ads healthcare
  review is a minefield and our own hard rule is no clinical positioning.
  Organic guides someday, with the doctor-first framing; never ads.
- **Moving savings to a riskier portfolio** · financial-advice adjacency
  we've excluded everywhere else on the site. Not worth the exception.
- **Egg freezing** · fertility sits inside Google's personalised-ads
  health restrictions and deserves better than an ad click. The
  deciding-to-have-a-child page already serves the searcher organically.
- **Cutting contact with a relative who hurt you** · abuse-adjacent
  traffic must never meet an ad. The crisis boundary in every page's copy
  exists precisely for whoever arrives here by another road.
