THE SOLO EDITION

How to make a big decision when the only vote is yours.

Counsel.day was built for two people, and it works exactly the same way for one. You cast a single sealed vote each evening, for a duration you set, and the record opens on the final day. Your first Solo decision is free, no card required.

THE MECHANISM, UNCHANGED

One sealed vote each evening, for a duration you choose.

Nothing about the mechanism changes when there is only one of you. You write the question as a single sentence, usually beginning Should I, and you pick a duration between seven and ninety days. Each evening, at a time you set, one prompt arrives. You tap one of four buttons: Strong yes, Lean yes, Lean no, Strong no. You can add a short note, typed or spoken.

The vote seals into the database within milliseconds. You cannot read yesterday's answer back mid-period, and neither can we. On the final evening the whole record opens at once: every vote, every note, and the shape your conviction took.

There is no second person to wait for, so none of the pacing is negotiated. You set the duration on day one, and you can extend it later, up to ninety days; you cannot shorten it.

WHAT PEOPLE BRING ALONE

The questions that arrive without a second voice.

Whether to leave the job that is wearing you down. Whether to move, to another city or another country, when nobody else is packing. Whether to end something that has been ending slowly for a year, or to stay through one more season of it. Whether to commit to a course of study that will cost you two years and a great deal of money.

And the health-adjacent ones. Whether to accept the surgery a specialist has already offered you. Whether to stop drinking. Whether to start the training you have been talking about since last winter. These are lifestyle questions you have been circling, not medical ones. Counsel.day records what you think about them, night after night, and hands the record back to you. It does not advise you, and it is not a replacement for the professionals who do.

Duration follows the deadline. Fourteen to twenty-one days when something external is forcing the timing; thirty to sixty when the question is genuinely open.

WHY THE TIME SERIES STILL MATTERS

You are comparing yourself against yourself.

A decision made alone usually gets made on a single evening, in a single mood, often a tired one. Tuesday's answer and Sunday's answer are frequently two different people's answers. Deciding alone is not really a shortage of advice; it is a shortage of perspective on your own state.

Voting once a night across a period turns one reading into a series. Thirty evenings of your own answers is a different class of evidence from one evening's. You get to see whether your position is firming, drifting, or holding steady, and whether the yes you felt on a good Friday survives an ordinary Wednesday. You are not trusting the mood you happen to be in tonight. You are reading yourself across many of them.

It also settles the other failure mode of deciding alone: the endless re-litigating. The question has one place to go, once a day. The rest of the day is yours.

FREE, AND WHAT PAYING ADDS

What the free first decision includes.

Your first Solo decision is free and needs no card. The free tier gives you the numerical summary: the chart of your own trajectory across the period, your full vote record, and every note you wrote handed back to you. That is the raw shape of the decision, and for a lot of questions it is all you need.

IF IT TURNS OUT NOT TO BE YOURS ALONE

A solo decision can become a shared one.

Sometimes you open a question by yourself and work out, a week in, that it was never only yours. You can invite someone into a Solo decision: add one person and it becomes a Couple decision, add two or more and it becomes a Family decision, up to six participants. Their voting starts when they join. Your own record from the days before stays intact.

Because that upgrade moves you onto a paid tier, the invitation does not leave until payment has cleared. Nothing is sent to anyone else in your name before that, and the question stays between the participants either way.

THE BOUNDARY

What this is, and what it is not.

Counsel.day is a data tool. It records what you tell it, counts it, and shows you the pattern. It is not a therapy service, a medical service, or a counselling service, and it is not validated, built, tested, or endorsed by clinicians. James Graham, who built it, is a data professional, not a therapist. Nothing here treats, assesses, or diagnoses anything.

It is made for a decision you have the time to weigh over weeks. If the question you are carrying involves your safety, or a mental-health concern, that is not something to put on a thirty-day timer, and this is not the right tool for it. Contact a qualified local service instead: Lifeline NZ on 0800 543 354, Samaritans UK on 116 123, or your national equivalent. They are better at this than any product is, and they answer tonight.
