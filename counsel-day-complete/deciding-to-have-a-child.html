USE CASE 01 · FAMILY & REPRODUCTION

Deciding whether to have a child.

Whether to have a first child. Whether to have another. Whether to pursue fertility treatment, whether to adopt, whether to stop trying. The honest answer to any of them moves from week to week, and a single conversation can only record the week it happened in. Counsel.day takes one private vote from each of you, every evening, for a duration you set, and reports the whole period back on the final day.

THE QUESTIONS IN SCOPE

Five versions of the same question.

Whether to have a first child. Whether to have another, now that you know what the first one costs in sleep and money and attention. Whether to pursue fertility treatment, and for how long. Whether to adopt. Whether to stop trying.

They are different questions with one thing in common: the answer is not a fact either of you can look up. Two people can be completely honest on the same evening and still say different words. You write the question as a single sentence, usually beginning Should we, and it stays fixed for the whole duration. Fixing the wording is most of the work. Should we try for a second child and should we try for a second child this year produce different periods.

WHY ONE CONVERSATION IS NOT ENOUGH

The answer changes with the week.

You already know this. The answer is different after a weekend with friends who have a newborn. Different in the third week of a hard project. Different when you are rested and different when you are not, different in the days after a month that did not work out. None of that is inconsistency. It is what a decision of this size actually looks like from the inside.

A conversation over dinner captures one evening of it. It also captures whoever is more articulate that night, and whoever speaks first sets the frame the other one then has to argue against. The sealed format takes both of those out. Each of you answers on your own, the answer seals into the database within milliseconds, and neither of you can read the other's position until the final day. You can see that your partner has voted tonight. You cannot see which way they leaned.

THE DURATION

Ninety days, for this one.

Durations run from seven to ninety days. For reproduction questions we recommend the long end: ninety days. A fortnight records a mood. Three months crosses enough of them that a pattern in the data is a pattern rather than a bad week.

Sixty days is a reasonable second choice when there is a real date in front of you, such as an appointment, a lease, or a birthday you have quietly set as a limit. You can extend a running decision up to the ninety-day maximum if the timeline shifts; you cannot shorten it, and that is deliberate. Missing an evening costs nothing. The verdict runs on the votes that sealed.

WHAT THE FINAL DAY PRODUCES

Three months of your own words, read back.

On the last evening both verdicts publish at the same instant, so neither answer is shaped by hearing the other one first. Underneath them sits the analysis of the period.

Each partner's trajectory. The shape of your conviction across the whole duration: firming toward yes, drifting toward no, or holding flat. A position that rose steadily across ninety evenings is different information from the same answer given once at a table.

The themes your notes kept returning to. Drawn from what you actually wrote, not from the reasons you predicted on day one. Money and timing usually appear. So do the ones that are harder to say out loud in a conversation: work, a parent's health, whose life changes more.

Where you actually diverged. Two people can both vote yes for a whole season and still want different things. The synthesis paragraph names the axis underneath the votes, and the period closes with one concrete question to open the conversation that follows. The product decides nothing for you. It records what each of you thought, on each day, and hands it back.

HOW TO RUN IT

On your own first, or together.

Your first Solo decision is free, with no card. That is the cheapest way to see the format before you ask anyone else to spend three months inside it; Solo decisions after that are $4.99 USD each. When both of you are voting, run it as a Couple decision at $9.99 USD, and your partner joins through a link sent to their inbox. The question lives only between the participants.

Each evening you tap one of four answers and, if a thought has landed, add a short note. The note can be typed or spoken; the microphone transcribes up to thirty seconds a day. Much of the useful material arrives that way, in half a sentence on the walk home.

THE BOUNDARY

What this is not.

Counsel.day is a data tool. It records what each person thought, over time, and reports it back. It is not therapy, counselling, or medical care. It is not validated, built, tested, or endorsed by clinicians, and the founder is a data professional, not a therapist. Nothing here is medical advice: questions about fertility treatment, or about whether to keep going, belong with your own doctor.

It is built for a decision you have time to weigh, not for a crisis. If the question sits next to a safety concern, abuse, or a mental-health crisis, please contact a qualified local service rather than opening a ninety-day decision: Lifeline NZ on 0800 543 354, Samaritans UK on 116 123, or your national equivalent.
